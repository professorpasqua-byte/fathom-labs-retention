the starting point

A resignation, used as a question rather than a verdict

In September 2026, an AI lab researcher resigned publicly, citing fear that the industry's pace leaves too little room for safety work. It wasn't an isolated case — the same tension keeps surfacing across the field.

This case study doesn't diagnose any one person's reasons for leaving. It tests the pattern underneath it, on a fictional lab and a synthetic population: does workload actually predict who leaves — or is something else doing the real work?

ask

What organizational factors predict a safety researcher leaving, and can a company act before it reaches a public resignation?

prepare

850-researcher synthetic population, built with Python Faker: tenure, role, workload, and five psychological measures.

analyze

Deduplicated and imputed in SQL, then a standardized logistic regression fit in scikit-learn to rank the real predictors.

share

An interactive Streamlit app where anyone can simulate an organizational change and watch predicted attrition move.

act

Three recommendations below, grounded in which factors the model actually found, not asserted from opinion.

The two strongest predictors are psychological, not operational: whether people believe raising a concern leads anywhere, and whether they believe the work itself is causing harm. Raw workload hours barely move the needle.

what this suggests

Three moves grounded in the model, not opinion

01

Build an escalation path with visible follow-through

Not an open-door policy — a record of concerns raised and what changed because of them. The gap between "I can speak up" and "I've seen it work" is what the model is actually measuring.

02

Treat moral injury as distinct from burnout

A wellness stipend doesn't touch it. It requires giving people real say over release pace or scope — the belief that the work itself causes harm isn't solved by resting more.

03

Stop relying on equity vesting as the retention lever

The model shows it's weak here — someone who believes the work is doing harm isn't meaningfully held by sunk-cost economics, which matches real cases of people forfeiting unvested equity to leave anyway.

Fathom Labs, every researcher record, and every figure above are synthetic — generated with Python Faker to carry the kind of noise a real workforce export has. No real individual's data appears anywhere in this project. Full pipeline, dataset generator, and live interactive version on GitHub.
